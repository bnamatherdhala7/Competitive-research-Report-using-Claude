Acrobat CTA & Hub Page — Competitive Study

Design Research · April 2026 · Adobe Internal · Responding to Nathan's redesign feedback

The Two Design Questions

Nathan's feedback raises two distinct UX decisions for Acrobat. Each has a clear answer in competitor data.

Question 1 — Hub Page CTA

Should the hero CTA on hub pages be a specific premium product push ("Try Acrobat Studio") or a generic free-entry point ("Try Acrobat for free" → plans page) that lets users self-select?

Question 2 — Task-Based Navigation

Should product/offering pages expose task-based pathways (Merge PDF, PDF to Word, Compress…) like competitors, in addition to the core capabilities grid?

Recommendation — What Acrobat Should Do

Two changes, both supported by competitor data. Neither requires new product features — these are entirely CTA and page structure decisions.

✗ Current State — What to Change
  • "Try Acrobat Studio" as the hub page hero CTA Pushes unknown visitors into the most expensive product before they self-select. Matches Foxit's pattern — the only paid-first competitor in the top 5.
  • No task-based pathways on hub or offering pages Users who want to "merge a PDF" have no direct entry — they see a capabilities pitch instead. iLovePDF and PDF24 have zero friction here.
  • "Get Acrobat Pro" as the primary CTA on the plans page Leads with the expensive option. iLovePDF and Smallpdf both lead with free-tier CTAs — they capture the broadest audience first.
  • No free tier prominently anchored at the start of the plan sequence The free tier exists but is visually subordinate. Every free-first competitor puts it first in the left-to-right plan hierarchy.
✓ Recommended State — Nathan's Direction + Competitive Data
  • Change hub page hero CTA to "Try Acrobat for free" → plans page Generic free-first entry point. Users self-select their plan. Matches 4 of 5 competitors. Apply to all customer group hubs as Nathan requested.
  • Add a task-grid section below the hero fold 8–10 most-used tasks: Edit PDF, Sign PDF, Merge PDF, PDF to Word, Compress, PDF to Excel, Protect, OCR. Mirrors Smallpdf's below-fold pattern (less extreme than iLovePDF but same benefit).
  • Make "Start free trial" the primary CTA on all plans except the top tier Matches iLovePDF and Smallpdf. Capture the broadest funnel at the plans page — let the product convert free users to paid.
  • Demote "Buy now" to outline/secondary on top-tier plan only Reserve direct purchase CTA for visitors who have already decided. All others get trial-first path. Nathan's exact request — supported by 4/5 competitors.
The evidence for Nathan's position is clear: 4 of 5 top competitors lead with free access on their hub pages. The one exception — Foxit — is the competitor that positions itself as "The #1 Alternative to Adobe Acrobat" and is the only one still using purchase-first language. Adobe's current pattern aligns with the challenger, not the leaders.

On task-based navigation: iLovePDF and PDF24 have the highest free-tier acquisition volume in the category precisely because their homepage removes all friction between a user's intent ("I need to merge a PDF") and the action. Smallpdf's hybrid approach (hero + task grid below fold) is the right middle ground for a brand that still needs to communicate quality and trust — it doesn't sacrifice brand positioning to lead with tools.
